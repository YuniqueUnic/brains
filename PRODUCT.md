# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People and AI agents who keep long-lived notes, project knowledge, and working documents. They need to create and find files quickly, keep a clear revision history, and move their knowledge between storage backends without making it depend on one application.

## Product Purpose

Brains is a human-centered, portable knowledge workspace. It stores ordinary files and preserves every published revision, so users can work with familiar documents while retaining recoverable history. Success means that writing, finding, restoring, and moving knowledge feel direct, and that the files remain useful outside Brains.

## Brand Personality

Calm, dense, keyboard-first. The interface gives experienced users quick access to actions while keeping the document and its current state easy to understand.

## Anti-references

- Traditional admin dashboards that give every control equal weight or keep low-value information permanently beside the document.
- Cluttered screens with repeated controls, similar but inconsistent components, or actions detached from their selected items.
- Destructive actions without clear feedback and a practical recovery path.
- Decorative motion or visual effects that compete with reading and editing.

## Design Principles

- Keep the document and its revision state as the main focus; reveal contextual details when they are useful.
- Make common actions direct and keyboard-accessible, with the same behavior and feedback across lists and panels.
- Show loading, saving, conflicts, and failures clearly, then provide a readable next action or recovery path.
- Reuse shared interface primitives while keeping feature-specific rules with the feature that owns them.
- Keep layout calm and information-dense; use visual hierarchy to make the next useful action apparent.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Core flows must work with a keyboard and communicate their state to screen readers. Keep visible focus, do not use color as the only status signal, respect reduced-motion preferences, and keep controls usable at narrow viewport widths and increased interface scale.
